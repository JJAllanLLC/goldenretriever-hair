# Golden Retriever Puppy Care

</div>

The first year is a health year. Vaccines, deworming, what goes in the bowl, and how fast the skeleton is allowed to grow decide more of the adult dog than any one afternoon of tricks. Training still matters. Sit, down, stay, come, housetraining, bite inhibition, and socialization practice live on the [puppy training hub](/guides/puppy-training). This page is the veterinary side of the same puppy: the visit schedule, the parasites, the growth diet, sleep, teeth, coat handling, and the exercise a large sporting puppy can actually do.

A Golden sits on the large-breed line. The [AKC breed page](https://www.akc.org/dog-breeds/golden-retriever/) lists males at 65 to 75 pounds and 23 to 24 inches, and females at 55 to 65 pounds and 21.5 to 22.5 inches. [VCA's large- and giant-breed nutrition page](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/nutritional-considerations-for-large-and-giant-breed-dogs) uses 70 pounds or more at adulthood as its line for large and giant dogs. Many males land on that line. Many females finish under it. This page will not call every Golden a giant, and it will not hand you one month when the growth plates close.

## The first veterinary visit

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Bring the records. Start the series around six to eight weeks. Plan on a visit every three to four weeks while the puppy is growing fast.</div>

[AVMA's page on selecting a pet dog](https://www.avma.org/resources/pet-owners/petcare/selecting-pet-dog) says to arrange an exam as soon as possible and to bring any vaccination and deworming history. Puppies need more housetraining, socialization, obedience work, feeding, exercise, and supervision than adults. The critical socialization period on that page is 3 to 14 weeks. The practice itself, including outings before the series is finished, is the [socialization guide](/guides/golden-retriever-socialization-guide) and the training hub.

[VCA's recommendations for new puppy owners](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/puppy---recommendations-for-new) says most puppies are seen at six to eight weeks to start vaccines and parasite preventives. The [2019 AAHA new-puppy checklist](https://www.aaha.org/wp-content/uploads/globalassets/02-guidelines/canine-life-stage-2019/caninelifestage_checklists_newpuppy.pdf) calls the puppy stage birth through the end of rapid growth, about 6 to 9 months depending on breed and size, with a visit every 3 to 4 weeks. Those visits cover breed risks, grooming, teeth, what and how often the puppy eats, exercise, a microchip, puppy-proofing, a car restraint, and fear. The [first week home guide](/guides/golden-retriever-first-week-home) is the arrival week. The same checklist says to discuss the best age to spay or neuter, with benefits and risks. It prints no Golden month. The [arthritis guide](/guides/golden-retriever-arthritis) is the joint side of that conversation.

## Vaccines, and why the charts do not match

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Core protection is a series, finished at 16 weeks or later. Leptospirosis is urged for most North American dogs and still labeled noncore on AAHA's table. The clinic picks the plan.</div>

[AVMA's vaccination page](https://www.avma.org/resources-tools/pet-owners/petcare/vaccinations) says the veterinarian builds an individualized plan. For the first months of life, that page describes a series starting at 6 to 8 weeks. For most puppies the final dose in the series is given at 16 weeks of age or older, and the veterinarian may adjust for the individual. Skipping or delaying recommended vaccinations can leave protection incomplete.

The [2022 AAHA canine vaccination guidelines](https://www.aaha.org/wp-content/uploads/globalassets/02-guidelines/2022-aaha-canine-vaccination-guidelines/resources/2022-aaha-canine-vaccinations-guidelines.pdf) separate core vaccines, for all dogs unless there is a medical reason not to vaccinate, from noncore vaccines chosen by lifestyle and place. The task force agreed on core. It did not always agree on noncore, and the veterinarian has discretion. Table 2's core antigens are distemper, adenovirus, parvovirus, with or without parainfluenza, plus rabies as the law requires.

For a dog 16 weeks or younger, that table asks for at least three doses of a combination vaccine between 6 and 16 weeks, 2 to 4 weeks apart. A dog older than 16 weeks at the start gets two doses, 2 to 4 weeks apart. Revaccination is one dose within a year of the last puppy dose, then boosters every three years. The distemper and parvovirus narrative explains why the series is long. Maternal antibodies can block a modified-live vaccine. They usually fade by 12 to 14 weeks, sometimes later, so AAHA says to revaccinate every 2 to 4 weeks until the puppy is older than 16 weeks. Where distemper risk is high, 18 to 20 weeks may be the preferred finish. Rabies follows local law.

Leptospirosis is where AAHA's text splits. Table 2 lists a killed four-serovar vaccine as noncore: two doses, 2 to 4 weeks apart, from 12 weeks, then yearly. The narrative says to strongly consider it for most dogs in North America. [VCA's owner page](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/puppy---recommendations-for-new) groups it with the core diseases. Distemper, hepatitis, and parvovirus are generally three injections, about 3 to 4 weeks apart, from 6 to 8 weeks through 16 weeks or later. Leptospirosis may share that shot or take two of its own. Rabies is often at 12 to 16 weeks. Bordetella, Lyme, and influenza follow where the dog lives. Swimming and courtyard life are different exposures: the [swimming guide](/guides/golden-retriever-swimming-guide) and the [apartment living guide](/guides/golden-retriever-apartment-living-guide). The clinic writes the card.

AAHA says no vaccine produces complete immunity, and it tells clinics to report an adverse event to the manufacturer and to the USDA Center for Veterinary Biologics. If the puppy is not themselves after a vaccine, call the clinic that gave it. This page lists no home treatment. A quiet puppy with vomiting or watery stool is the [diarrhea guide](/guides/golden-retriever-diarrhea-guide), including parvovirus in a puppy who is still in the series.

## Worms, fleas, and the preventives you do not dose at home

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Bring a stool sample. Deworming is repeated because one dose kills adult worms. The clinic chooses the product. This page names no drug and no dose.</div>

VCA's new-owner page says intestinal worms are common. Some arrive before birth or through milk. A fecal exam is recommended at the early visits, because an infected puppy may not pass visible worms every day. The clinic may give a dewormer for that reason. Some of these parasites can pass to people. Repeat the treatment: it kills adult worms, and larvae become adults in about three to four weeks. Hookworms, whipworms, and roundworms can reinfect a dog for life.

[VCA's 8-week page](https://vcahospitals.com/pediatric/puppy/health-wellness/8-week-old-puppy-care) says to bring a fresh stool sample and to wash your hands after handling feces, because hookworms and roundworms can reach people. The main dewormer at those visits does not treat tapeworms. Tapeworms usually follow a swallowed flea, or sometimes a rodent. Segments look like rice or cucumber seeds. Tell the veterinarian. A second product from the store is a guess.

AAHA's checklist puts intestinal parasites, heartworm prevention, and flea and tick prevention on the every-three-to-four-week list. [VCA's parasite-preventive page](https://vcahospitals.com/pediatric/puppy/health-wellness/puppy-vaccinations) says year-round cover for heartworms, fleas, ticks, and intestinal worms, as one product or several, chosen with the clinic. This page names no drug and no dose. A rice-like segment, a dull coat with a big belly, or a puppy who is not gaining is a call, and so is a breeder deworming that never came with a fecal result you can hand over.

## Feeding a Golden who is still growing

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Use a growth diet with an AAFCO statement that names large-size dogs. Measure it on a scale. Keep a puppy closer to a body condition score of 4 out of 9.</div>

[VCA's large- and giant-breed page](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/nutritional-considerations-for-large-and-giant-breed-dogs) is for dogs who will reach 70 pounds or more. Overfeeding can speed growth even when the puppy does not look fat, and the skeleton may not keep up. The [feeding growing puppies page](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/feeding-growing-puppies) ties free-choice feeding to extra body fat and, in large-breed puppies, skeletal abnormalities. Measured meals, generally two or three times a day, follow body condition and age. Moderate energy restriction during growth can lower hip dysplasia risk without changing adult size.

Puppies absorb extra calcium an adult would pass, and excess can malform the skeleton. Growth foods for large and giant breeds keep calcium and phosphorus tighter. VCA says a ratio of about 1.1 to 1 to 1.4 to 1 is usually safe if the puppy is not overeating. That describes the food. It is not a powder to add. High protein raises phosphorus and then calcium, so VCA prefers a moderate protein level. EPA and DHA belong in the food. Skip vitamin and mineral supplements unless the veterinarian asks. [AAHA's 2021 nutrition guidelines](https://www.aaha.org/wp-content/uploads/globalassets/02-guidelines/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines/2021-aaha-nutrition-and-weight-management-guidelines.pdf) say the same for giant-breed growth: stay lean, and avoid excess calcium.

The label to read is an AAFCO statement for growth that includes large-size dogs. VCA and the [Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/management-and-nutrition/nutrition-small-animals/feeding-practices-in-small-animals) both use about 70 pounds, or 32 kilograms, as that adult line. VCA accepts three wordings: a feeding trial, a food formulated to the AAFCO profiles, or a food comparable to one that passed a trial. All three are described as safe, and most foods never go through a feeding trial. A food labeled for all life stages can also suit growth. Typical adult food is the switch to avoid until the puppy reaches adult weight. How many months that takes is the disagreement in the table.

The 24-month figure is for giants. The clinic picks the month for this Golden. VCA's Waltham charts do not fit giant breeds. AAHA's puppy stage, about 6 to 9 months, is fast growth. Skeletal maturity is later.

The [WSAVA chart](https://wsava.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/WSAVA_BCSCat_BCSDog_Nutrition_250612.pdf) is a 9-point scale. Scores 4 and 5 are ideal: ribs you can feel, a waist from above, a tuck from the side. VCA says puppies are safer at 4 out of 9. Weigh weekly. Merck wants a small gain and the slower rate. Puppies held to 50 to 70 percent of free-choice intake did not finish shorter, or short on bone or muscle. Body fat is what changed. Measure meals on a kitchen scale. Cups drift. Keep extra foods out while the puppy is growing. Adults may get up to 10 percent of calories from treats. The [weight guide](/guides/golden-retriever-weight-obesity) and the [puppy feeding chart](/guides/golden-retriever-puppy-feeding-schedule-chart) sit beside the clinic's number. Ribs win.

That page links bloat with one meal a day, fast eating, an elevated bowl, water restricted around meals, moistened kibble, a single food form, and stress. Feed at least four small meals, leave kibble dry, use a slow bowl on the ground, and skip hard exercise right before or after eating. The [bloat guide](/guides/golden-retriever-bloat-gdv) is the emergency. A slow bowl changes pace.

## Sleep

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Plan on a puppy who is asleep most of the day. The two AKC pages do not print the same hour count. Do not wake a nap to tire the puppy out.</div>

[AKC's sleep page](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/how-much-do-puppies-sleep/) says puppies usually sleep 18 to 20 hours a day, in naps of about 30 minutes to two hours, and to take them out when they wake. [AKC's schedule article](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/training/setting-schedules-and-developing-a-routine-for-your-new-puppy/) says some puppies sleep as much as 16 to 18 hours, and that a sleeping puppy should be left alone. Both numbers are published. An overtired puppy is the one who will not settle, which is a reason to start the nap sooner.

[ASPCA's general care page](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/dog-care/general-dog-care) wants a warm, quiet rest place off the floor, and bedding that gets washed. [AKC's crate article](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/training/how-to-crate-train-a-puppy) sizes the crate so the dog can stand, turn, and lie down, with a divider for a puppy, and says to stop if the dog panics: pacing, shaking, chewing the door, long howling, drooling. Teaching the crate, and how long a Golden can be alone, is the training hub and the [alone-time guide](/guides/golden-retriever-alone-time-by-age). Age totals are the [puppy sleep chart](/guides/golden-retriever-puppy-sleep-chart). Night waking is [why a puppy will not sleep](/guides/golden-retriever-puppy-wont-sleep). Adults and seniors are the [sleeping guide](/guides/golden-retriever-sleeping-guide). The arrival week is the [first-week schedule](/guides/golden-retriever-first-week-home-schedule-chart).

A puddle on waking is timing. The method is the [puppy training hub](/guides/puppy-training) and the [potty schedule chart](/guides/golden-retriever-potty-training-schedule-chart). [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/news/house-training-your-dog-or-puppy) says a scolding for a puddle you found later does not teach the spot. A puppy who had been reliable and then strains, passes blood, or goes quiet is a same-day call.

## Teeth

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Baby teeth are in by about two months. Adult teeth arrive across three to eight months. A baby tooth still beside an adult tooth goes to the veterinarian. Do not pull it.</div>

The [teething and nipping page](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/puppy-teething-and-nipping/) says baby teeth can appear by two weeks, adult teeth start around three to four months, and baby teeth should be gone by about six months: 28, then 42. The [timeline](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/timeline-of-puppy-teething/) puts the baby set in by five to six weeks and the first lost teeth around 12 to 16 weeks. The [tooth-count page](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/how-many-teeth-do-dogs-have/) finishes the baby set a little later, then incisors near four months, canines near five to six months, and premolars and molars from five to eight months. A tooth on the floor can be normal. Jerry Klein says to tell the veterinarian if baby teeth remain. Do not pull them.

Drooling and a small blood spot on a toy can be teething, and that page says the same signs can be something else. Offer a chew that stays in one piece, and stay in the room. Start touching the mouth now, which the timeline page recommends, so brushing is possible later. Human toothpaste can contain xylitol. ## Exercise, stairs, and open growth plates

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Several short walks and ordinary play. Long runs, jumps, and stair sprints wait. A limp is a veterinary visit.</div>

The AKC breed page says a Golden needs plenty of daily exercise, and that a dog who misses it is likely to invent a project. The same paragraph mentions long runs and bike rides, and it says to ask a veterinarian before strenuous or high-impact work that could stress bones and joints. [AKC's exercise article](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/how-much-exercise-does-dog-need/) says the amount depends on age, health, and breed, and that puppies need several short sessions. A long one is hard on a body that is still growing. Mental work complements the outing. ASPCA says exercise needs vary, and that boredom feeds destructive behavior.

[VCA's growth-plate page](https://vcahospitals.com/pediatric/puppy/health-wellness/puppy-exercise) says to wait on long jogs, long hikes, jumps, and competitive agility until the puppy is mature. That may be a little past six months in a small dog and closer to a year and a half in a giant. A Golden is large. Neither VCA nor this page names the month a Golden's plates close. Walks and self-directed play are allowed. The puppy sets the pace. A limp, or a change in how the puppy sits or stands, is a clinic visit.

Krontveit and colleagues (American Journal of Veterinary Research, 2012) followed Newfoundlands, Labrador Retrievers, Leonbergers, and Irish Wolfhounds. Stair use from birth to three months was tied to higher radiographic hip dysplasia risk. Off-leash work on soft ground in that window went the other way. Goldens were not in the study. The [apartment living guide](/guides/golden-retriever-apartment-living-guide) is the stair page: carry a very young puppy, and skip stair sprints. [VCA](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/hip-dysplasia-in-dogs) names Goldens among commonly affected large breeds. Signs include hind-end weakness, trouble rising, a limp, and reluctance to climb, sometimes in a puppy and more often at one to two years. The [hip dysplasia guide](/guides/golden-retriever-hip-dysplasia), the arthritis guide, and the [exercise needs guide](/guides/golden-retriever-exercise-needs) continue from here. The AKC prints no minute count.

## The rest of the checklist

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Spay or neuter timing, a microchip, a car restraint, and puppy-proofing are clinic conversations. Training methods stay on the training hub.</div>

AAHA's checklist asks the clinic to cover spay or neuter timing, a microchip with a current phone number, hazards, a car restraint, and positive reinforcement. The [car travel guide](/guides/golden-retriever-car-travel-guide) is the restraint. The training hub is the reinforcement, the housetraining method, the leash, and the short sessions. Supplements are on that same discuss list. VCA's answer, unless the veterinarian has asked, is to leave vitamin and mineral powders out. A joint chew for a growing Golden is that conversation. The arthritis guide is for a dog who already needs joint care.

## When to call the vet

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Same day: a quiet puppy, vomiting, watery or bloody stool, a limp, labored breathing, or a belly that blows up. A single puddle after a late trip outside is training.</div>

Call the same day for any of these:

- Vomiting, watery or bloody stool, or a puppy who is quiet and will not eat. The diarrhea guide covers that pattern, including parvovirus during the vaccine series.
- A limp, or a change in how the puppy sits or stands. VCA treats open growth plates as something hard work can injure.
- Hind-end weakness, trouble rising, or a puppy who stops climbing. VCA lists those as hip dysplasia signs, including in young dogs.
- A hard, swelling abdomen after a meal, with restlessness or retching. That is the bloat guide and an emergency clinic.
- Rice-like segments, or a puppy who is not gaining on the weekly weigh-in. Bring a stool sample. Add no second dewormer yourself.
- A baby tooth beside an adult tooth, or mouth bleeding beyond a small spot on a toy.
- A puppy who is not themselves after a vaccine. Tell the clinic that gave it.
- Crate panic: pacing, shaking, door chewing, long howling, drooling. Stop that plan. The training hub comes after pain is ruled out.

A bright puppy with a soft stool the day the food changed can go back to the growth diet the clinic already approved, and still deserves a call if it lasts.

## Questions owners ask

### When do Golden Retriever puppy vaccines finish?

AVMA says the final dose for most puppies is at 16 weeks or older, and the veterinarian may move it. AAHA asks for at least three combination doses between 6 and 16 weeks, continued until the puppy is older than 16 weeks, sometimes to 18 or 20 weeks where distemper risk is high. Leptospirosis is noncore on AAHA's table, strongly considered for most North American dogs in the narrative, and grouped with core diseases on VCA's owner page. A missed visit is a phone call.

### Does a Golden need large-breed puppy food?

If this puppy should reach about 70 pounds, use a growth diet whose AAFCO statement includes large-size dogs. AKC males are 65 to 75 pounds and females 55 to 65, so ask. Stay on it until the clinic calls the skeleton mature: Merck 10 to 16 months for large and giant breeds, AAHA closer to 15 to 16 months for some, VCA around 18 months for large breeds and 24 for giants. Leave extra calcium out.

### How many meals a day should a Golden puppy get?

Merck says three times a day until 6 months, then twice until 12 months. VCA's growing-puppy page says two or three measured meals. VCA's large-breed page says at least four small meals, from the ground, with dry food left dry. The veterinarian sets the number. Free-choice feeding is the plan VCA ties to extra fat and skeletal abnormalities.

### How much do Golden puppies sleep?

AKC's sleep article says 18 to 20 hours, in naps of about 30 minutes to two hours. AKC's schedule article says some puppies sleep as much as 16 to 18 hours. Take them out when they wake, and leave a sleeping puppy asleep. The sleep chart and the night-waking guide split the rest. Panic in the crate is a reason to stop and call.

### When do puppy teeth fall out?

Baby teeth can start by two weeks and should be gone by about six months, with adult teeth arriving from about three to eight months depending on which AKC page you read. There are about 28 baby teeth and 42 adult teeth. A baby tooth still beside an adult tooth is a veterinary visit. A small blood spot on a toy can be teething, and unusual bleeding can be something else.

### How much exercise is safe before the growth plates close?

Several short walks and free play, with the puppy setting the pace. VCA waits on long runs, jumps, and competitive agility until maturity, a little past six months in small dogs and closer to a year and a half in giants. A Golden is large, and this page will not invent the closing month. A limp ends the outing. Stair repeats are a poor workout. The apartment guide has the hip study, which did not include Goldens.

### When should a Golden be spayed or neutered?

AAHA's checklist says to discuss the best age, and the benefits and risks, with the veterinarian. It publishes no Golden Retriever age. The arthritis guide is the joint side of that talk. Book it at a puppy visit.
