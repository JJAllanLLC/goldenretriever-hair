# Overtired Golden Retriever Puppy: How to Spot It and Settle Them

</div>

An overtired Golden Retriever puppy rarely looks sleepy. It looks wild: harder bites, zoomies, barking at nothing, and a puppy who ignores every cue it knew an hour ago. The fix is usually less activity, not more. Young puppies are built to sleep most of the day. The [American Kennel Club](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/training/setting-schedules-and-developing-a-routine-for-your-new-puppy/) says some sleep 16 to 18 hours a day and need quiet naps several times a day, and [PetMD](https://www.petmd.com/dog/general-health/puppy-sleep-schedule) gives a range of 12 to 20 hours.

This page is the how-to for the overtired puppy: the signs, why it happens, a nap routine that prevents it, and what to do in the middle of a meltdown. Hours by age are in the [puppy sleep chart](/guides/golden-retriever-puppy-sleep-chart). Night waking and crying in the crate are in [puppy won't sleep at night](/guides/golden-retriever-puppy-wont-sleep).

## Why it happens

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Puppies need far more sleep than adults, and a busy house keeps waking them up.</div>

PetMD says puppies sleep 12 to 20 hours a day and alternate sleep with bursts of energy. AKC says family members, especially young children, should learn not to disturb a sleeping puppy, and that a crate or pen in a quieter spot helps them nap away from the bustle of the house. The usual causes of an overtired Golden are simple:

- **Too long awake.** Back-to-back play, training, and visitors with no break.
- **Skipped or broken naps.** A puppy carried around or woken by children.
- **Too much excitement.** New places, other dogs, rough play, or a long car ride.
- **No settled place to sleep.** A puppy that only sleeps on laps does not learn to rest alone.

Goldens are social and food-motivated, so they will keep going as long as someone keeps playing. That is why the routine has to come from you.

## Build a settle and nap routine

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Wake, potty, play or train, potty, then rest. Repeat through the day.</div>

AKC recommends a regular routine with potty trips at least every two to four hours and after every change of activity, and planned quiet naps several times a day. A simple loop works for most young Goldens:

1. **Wake.** Straight outside for a potty break.
2. **Food or water** if it is mealtime.
3. **Short play or training.** A few minutes of fetch, tug, or a short training session.
4. **Potty again.**
5. **Wind down.** Lead the puppy to its crate or pen with a chew or a stuffed toy.
6. **Nap.** Leave it in a quiet room. Let it sleep until it wakes on its own.

VCA describes a crate as a safe place associated with rest, like a child's playpen, and suggests making it a place for quiet enjoyment. Never use it as a punishment.

## Calm-down protocol for a meltdown

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Stop, potty, quiet, chew, rest. Keep your voice low and your hands still.</div>

1. **Stop the game.** Freeze, fold your arms, and stop talking. Do not push the puppy away, which looks like more play.
2. **Take it out to potty** on leash, calmly. Keep it boring.
3. **Lower the volume.** Dim lights, turn down the TV, ask children to step away for a few minutes.
4. **Offer something to chew or lick.** Chewing helps many puppies settle. A stuffed KONG works well.
5. **Lead it to the crate or pen** and close the door while it chews. If it cries, sit nearby quietly. PetMD suggests calm, quiet reassurance rather than excited interaction.
6. **Let it sleep.** Do not wake it to show visitors. When it wakes, go straight outside.

## Prevent the next one

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Schedule naps before the witching hour, not after it.</div>

- Put a nap on the calendar for late afternoon, before the usual evening frenzy.
- Keep training sessions short, and end on an easy win.
- After a big day out, plan a quiet day.
- Teach children to leave a sleeping puppy alone, as AKC advises.
- Give the puppy a daytime nap spot away from the busiest room.
- Puppy biting in general, not only when tired, is covered in the [puppy biting guide](/guides/golden-retriever-puppy-biting-guide).

## When to see a vet

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">A tired puppy perks up after rest. A sick puppy does not.</div>

Overtired behavior should improve after a good nap. Call your veterinarian if your puppy:

- Is unusually sleepy, weak, or hard to wake, or not interested in play after resting.
- Stops eating, vomits, or has diarrhea.
- Strains to pee, has frequent accidents after doing well, or wakes often to urinate.
- Seems in pain, limps, or cries when touched.
- Has a sudden change in behavior that does not fit the time of day.
- Growls, stiffens, or snaps in a way that seems different from play biting.

Go the same day, or to an emergency clinic, for collapse, trouble breathing, repeated vomiting, or a puppy you cannot rouse.

## Questions owners ask

### How can I tell if my Golden Retriever puppy is overtired?

It gets wilder rather than sleepier: harder biting, zoomies, barking, and ignoring cues, usually after a long stretch awake or a busy day. If it falls asleep quickly once it is in a quiet spot, it was overtired.

### Should I exercise an overtired puppy more?

No. More activity usually makes it worse. Try a potty break and a quiet nap first. A puppy that is truly under-exercised is calm after a nap and still restless.

### How long should a Golden Retriever puppy nap?

Let it sleep until it wakes on its own. AKC and PetMD both say young puppies sleep a large share of the day, so several long naps are normal.

### Are enforced naps good for puppies?

Planned naps in a crate or pen help many puppies, as long as the crate is a calm, positive place and never a punishment. VCA describes the crate as a safe place for rest.

### What is the puppy witching hour?

The early evening, when many puppies get wild. AKC uses the term and suggests giving a treat and letting the puppy settle in its crate if play does not calm it.

### Why does my puppy fight sleep?

It is often too wound up to switch off. A predictable routine, a quiet room, and something to chew help it settle.
