# Golden Retriever Ear Infections

</div>

A Golden Retriever's ear gets infected because the canal is built to hold moisture. The [American Kennel Club](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/common-conditions/dog-ear-infections/) describes a dog's ear canal as L-shaped, so fluid tends to stay in it, and it names floppy ears as a reason some dogs are at higher risk. [VCA Animal Hospitals](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/ear-infections-in-dogs-otitis-externa) calls an outer-ear infection, otitis externa, one of the most common infections in dogs, and it lists large, floppy, or hairy ears among the traits that raise the risk. The [Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/ear-disorders/otitis-externa/otitis-externa-in-animals) (published as the MSD Veterinary Manual) adds the breed context: ear-disease risk tracks skin-disease risk, and it names allergies in retrievers as that kind of pattern. A pendulous ear flap and water from swimming are on its list of factors that show up before the infection does.

This page is the cleaning guide and the scratching guide as well as the infection guide. It is not a diagnosis, and it does not include doses. Yeast, bacteria, and mites are not told apart by smell from the couch. [VCA](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/ear-infections-in-dogs-otitis-externa) is explicit that over-the-counter drops are the wrong first move, because the veterinarian has to know the organism and whether the eardrum is intact. Some medicines can damage hearing if the eardrum is already ruptured.

## Decide from the ear in front of you

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Wax on a comfortable ear is maintenance. Odor, pain, or head shaking is an appointment.</div>

[Cornell's Riney Canine Health Center](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-topics/how-clean-your-dogs-ears) says a healthy ear needs a routine clean only when dirt or debris is actually there, because overcleaning irritates the canal. Dogs with floppy ears, allergies, or a history of infections may need cleaning more often, and an ear that just got wet, after a swim or a bath, is a reason to dry it and clean if your veterinarian has already said cleaning is safe. [VCA's cleaning page](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/instructions-for-ear-cleaning-in-dogs) says some dogs with healthy ears never need a clean, and that discharge or odor is the cue to clean a quiet ear. It also says the schedule is your veterinarian's, not a calendar on the fridge.

## Why a Golden's ear stays wet

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">The flap covers an L-shaped canal. Moisture and allergy inflammation are what turn that shape into an infection.</div>

The part you can see is the pinna, the ear flap. The canal then runs down and turns inward toward the eardrum. That turn is the L the AKC describes, and it is why water and wax do not fall out on their own. Merck lists a pendulous pinna and excessive moisture, including swimming, as factors that are present before ear disease starts. Hair in and around the canal is on that same list. The AKC's practical version for a floppy ear is to have a groomer thin and trim hair in and around the opening, and to ask the veterinarian before anyone plucks hair from the canal. Merck calls plucking controversial: it is sometimes needed to treat an infection, and it is not a home grooming trick.

Allergy is the medical piece owners skip when they only dry the ears. VCA's figures are for dogs in general, not a Golden-only study: allergies are involved in as many as 43 percent of ear-infection cases, especially chronic or repeat ones, and ear infections occur in 65 to 80 percent of dogs with food allergies. The AKC's Dr. Hood says recurrent ear infections are often the first or only sign of a food or environmental allergy, because inflammation changes the wax, traps moisture, and lowers the local defenses. Merck lists allergy, including atopic dermatitis and adverse food reaction, as a primary cause, and Malassezia yeast plus bacteria such as Staphylococcus and Pseudomonas as the secondary overgrowth that follows. Hypothyroidism is on both VCA's and Merck's lists of hormone problems that can keep ears flaring. If the ears keep coming back after the drops, the next question is the skin, not a stronger cleaner. The on-site page is the [allergies guide](/guides/golden-retriever-allergies-guide).

## Constant scratching is a sign, not a habit

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Head shaking and pawing mean the canal is uncomfortable. The cause still has to be identified.</div>

VCA says ear infections hurt, and that dogs shake and scratch to try to clear debris and fluid. The AKC's symptom list is the one owners already know: head shaking, scratching or rubbing, dark discharge, odor, redness, swelling, pain, itch, and crusts or scabs. A single shake after a swim can be the dog clearing water. Scratching that continues, or shaking that is vigorous and repeated, is the AKC's cue to be seen.

Which ear matters. The AKC says problems in both ears often fit an allergy, because the trigger is affecting the whole dog, and a problem in only one ear makes that less likely. A sudden one-sided itch can be a foreign body, and VCA says a foreign body, polyp, or tumor will not resolve with medication alone. Grass awns show up in that category for dogs who have been in long grass. Do not dig for one.

Ear mites, Otodectes, are the other itchy pattern people reach for. VCA says the signs overlap with infection, a dark discharge plus scratching and head shaking, and that mites are more common in puppies and kittens. Adult dogs sometimes pick them up from an infected puppy or cat. Mites also set up a secondary bacterial or yeast infection, so the debris you see is not a label. The AKC says vet-approved monthly parasite control can take mites off the list as a cause of the itch. Confirmation is still the clinic's, from the exam and often the microscope.

## Yeast, bacteria, and mites

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Color and smell are clues for the vet. They are not a way to pick a bottle at home.</div>

The AKC defines the infection as inflammation of the canal with overgrowth of yeast, bacteria, or both. Dr. Hood says a dark discharge can be yeast, parasites, bacteria, or a mix, and that mixed infections are common enough that treatment often has to cover yeast and bacteria together. Merck names Malassezia as the usual yeast and lists several bacteria, including Staphylococcus and Pseudomonas. This guide does not give you a chart you can treat from. ## What the vet actually checks

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">The visit is an otoscope, a sample under the microscope, and a check that the eardrum is intact.</div>

VCA and the AKC describe the same workup. The veterinarian looks in the canal with an otoscope to see debris, foreign material, and whether the eardrum is whole. If the canal is too swollen, too full, or too painful, VCA says sedation or anesthesia may be needed before that look is even possible. Cytology, a stained sample of the discharge, is what chooses the drug. Culture and susceptibility testing is for severe or chronic cases, when the clinic needs to know which organism is present and which drug can reach it. Merck says samples should be taken before the ear is cleaned, so the flush does not wipe away the evidence.

Tell them about swims, baths, which cleaner you use, whether hair was plucked, and any drops already in the ear. The AKC lists those questions because they change what the canal looks like and what is safe to put in it next.

## Treatment stays with the clinic

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Drops, and sometimes oral medicine, are prescribed after the sample. This page has no doses.</div>

VCA says medication is chosen for the organism: antibiotics for bacteria, antifungals for fungi, and some products combine those with an anti-inflammatory. The AKC says the veterinarian usually cleans the ear with a medicated cleanser and sends topical drops home. Severe infections may add oral antibiotics and anti-inflammatory medicine. Yeast is cleaning plus an ear-specific antifungal, often combined with a steroid to bring swelling down. A mixed infection is treated for both at once. None of those sentences is a dose, a brand of prescription drop, or a number of days you can set yourself.

Finish the course and go back for the recheck. The AKC says most uncomplicated infections improve within one to two weeks once the right treatment has started, and that severe infections or those tied to another disease can take longer, sometimes months. Stopping early, Dr. Hood says, is how resistant infections get a start. Gaps and skipped rechecks are how an infection returns. If the ear seems better on day three, that is not evidence the organism is gone.

Do not reuse last month's bottle. The organism may be different, and the eardrum may not be in the same condition. The AKC's line is not to put home remedies or old medications in the ear without checking first, because they can irritate the canal or damage the eardrum.

Chronic ears can change shape. VCA says long inflammation can thicken the canal until it narrows, which is called stenosis, and medicine then cannot reach the horizontal canal. Some dogs need surgery, including removal of the vertical canal or, in some cases, the entire canal, and total ear canal ablation can leave permanent hearing loss. That is the outcome of disease that was not controlled. It is not a decision this guide walks you through.

If allergies are the primary cause Merck describes, the drops treat the overgrowth and the allergy plan is what reduces the next one. A food trial, when a vet suspects food, is the AKC's 8 to 12 week elimination diet, usually aimed at the protein source. Do not start that experiment on your own in the same week you are also changing ear drugs. The [skin problems guide](/guides/golden-retriever-skin-problems-guide) is the broader map when the coat and the ears are both involved.

## Clean a quiet ear. Leave a sore ear alone.

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Fill, massage, let the dog shake, and wipe only as far as a finger reaches. Stop if it hurts.</div>

[VCA's cleaning instructions](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/instructions-for-ear-cleaning-in-dogs) are the technique to use on an ear your veterinarian has said is safe to clean. The supplies are a cleaner the clinic is happy with, cotton balls or gauze, and treats. No cotton-tipped swab in the canal.

For a large dog, VCA has you sit the dog with its rear in a corner, one side against the wall, and you on the other side. Hold the flap straight up so the canal straightens. Squeeze cleaner in until the canal is full. Spill is fine. Do not bury the bottle tip in the ear. If the tip touches the ear, VCA says to wipe it with a cotton ball soaked in alcohol before the next ear, so you do not carry yeast or bacteria across. Massage the base for about 30 seconds. You should hear a squish as the solution moves in the horizontal canal. Wipe the flap and the upper canal with cotton or gauze, let the dog shake, then wipe again. Go into the canal only as far as your finger reaches. Praise, treat, and use a fresh cotton ball or piece of gauze on the other ear. If the dog shows pain, stop and call the clinic.

Cornell offers the same sequence and one alternative if your dog hates solution poured into the canal: saturate the cotton first, then massage. It also says that during treatment of a severe infection, home cleaning is often delayed for the first few days until medicine has taken the pain and swelling down. That delay is the veterinarian's call. So is any wait between a cleaner and a medicated drop. VCA warns that some cleansers weaken the medicine, so ask before you stack them. This page will not invent that interval.

How often is the part the old cleaning article made too rigid. Cornell says maintenance for a healthy ear is only when debris is present, and that a common maintenance interval when cleaning is needed is every one to two weeks. Floppy ears, allergies, and repeat infections can justify more. An ear being treated can be cleaned as often as daily at the start, on the clinic's schedule, not yours. VCA says overcleaning a healthy ear can itself cause the irritation that leads to infection. A weekly wipe of every Golden, infected or not, is not what either page says.

Hydrogen peroxide and alcohol are the cleaners VCA tells you to avoid, especially in an inflamed or ulcerated canal. Use a dog ear cleaner. The vet picks which one, because some are better at wax and some include antibacterial or antifungal ingredients aimed at prevention, not at replacing a prescription.

## After a swim

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Dry the flap and the opening after every swim or bath. Moisture is the part you can remove the same day.</div>

The AKC says excess moisture is a breeding ground for bacteria and yeast, and that floppy ears need extra care after a bath or a swim. Dry thoroughly. Do not pack the canal with a towel. Wipe the flap and the opening, then let the dog shake. Cornell recommends a routine clean when the ears get wet, if cleaning is appropriate for that dog. The [swimming guide](/guides/golden-retriever-swimming-guide) covers the water itself. Keeping shampoo and bath water out of the canal is the same idea on a bath day. The [grooming guide](/guides/golden-retriever-grooming-guide) and the [grooming schedule](/guides/golden-retriever-grooming-schedule) are where ear checks sit next to the rest of coat care.

A daily look is still worth doing on a dog who swims or who has had infections: odor, redness, discharge, or new head shaking. That look is not a clean. If any of those are present, you are back in the vet row of the table.

## Mistakes that make the next infection easier

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">The canal is injured by swabs, by overcleaning, and by drops that were never meant for this infection.</div>

A cotton swab in the canal is the expensive one. VCA and the AKC agree: it pushes debris deeper and can damage the eardrum. Swabs, if you use them at all, stay on the ear flap.

Overcleaning a healthy ear strips the protection the canal had. The AKC says cleaning too often removes protective oils and the bacteria that belong there, and that irritation can become an infection. VCA says the same. Match the clean to debris, wet ears, and the schedule your veterinarian set.

Cleaning or medicating an ear that is already painful spreads the problem and can put drug through a ruptured eardrum. VCA wants that ear examined first.

Stopping the drops when the head shaking eases, or restarting an old bottle because the smell seems familiar, skips the cytology. The AKC treats that as how resistance and recurrence happen.

Ignoring the other ear, or ignoring the skin, misses the pattern. Use a fresh cotton ball or gauze for each ear so you do not carry material across. If both ears and the skin are involved, ask about allergy rather than only buying more flush.

Plucking canal hair because a groomer did it once is the step Merck calls controversial. Ask before it happens again.

## When it is not ordinary wax

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Pain, a head tilt, a swollen flap, or trouble walking is urgent. Do not manage those at home.</div>

Call the clinic promptly for odor, discharge, redness, swelling, scratching or head shaking that is not a one-time shake, or an ear the dog will not let you touch. That is the AKC's "any of the common signs" list, and it is the point of cytology.

Go the same day, or to urgent care, if you see a head tilt, stumbling, walking in circles, or a sudden change in balance. The AKC says middle- and inner-ear disease can affect balance, and in severe cases hearing and the facial nerve. VCA says a deep infection can rupture the eardrum and lead to an inner-ear infection and permanent hearing loss.

A puffy ear flap after a day of hard shaking is an aural hematoma. VCA describes it as broken blood vessels in the flap, a painful swelling that needs surgical treatment. Blood from the canal, crying when the ear is touched, or an ear that is suddenly held flat belongs in that same urgent group. Do not clean it on the way to the car.
