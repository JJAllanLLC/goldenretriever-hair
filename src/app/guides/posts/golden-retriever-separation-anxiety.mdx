# Golden Retriever Separation Anxiety

</div>

A Golden Retriever who falls apart when you leave is having a welfare problem, not a spite problem. The [American Kennel Club](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/training/separation-anxiety-in-dogs), citing certified applied animal behaviorist Patricia McConnell, Ph.D., describes separation anxiety as extreme stress from the moment you leave until you come back, closer to a panic attack than to a shredded sock. The [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/dog-care/common-dog-behavior-issues/separation-anxiety) says the same pattern shows up as barking, howling, house soiling, chewing, digging, or escape attempts that start because the person the dog is attached to is gone. [VCA Animal Hospitals](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/dog-behavior-problems---separation-anxiety-in-dogs---synopsis) now uses the wider name separation-related distress, because some of these dogs are anxious, some are frustrated, and some are mostly restless. The useful test is still the one VCA gives: the behavior shows up when the dog is alone, and it rarely shows up when a valued companion is there.

Goldens are built to work next to a person. That bond is a breed trait, covered in the [temperament guide](/guides/golden-retriever-temperament), and it does not mean every Golden will panic at the door. It does mean alone time has to be taught. This page is the plan for teaching it, and the point at which home practice stops. It is not a diagnosis. It has no medication doses.

## Read the absence before you name it

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Film the dog. The video is the diagnosis, not the mess you find at 6 p.m.</div>

The AKC says destroyed cushions and a potty accident are not enough to name separation anxiety. The dog may not be fully house trained, or may not know the rules when nobody is watching. VCA wants an audio or video recording of the dog alone, because destruction, barking, and soiling that also happen while you are home point somewhere else: incomplete training, a medical problem, a noise outside, or confinement panic. The [VCA page on preventing separation distress](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/preventing-separation-distress-during-and-after-the-covid-19-pandemic) says a pet camera is one way to see pacing, panting, and restlessness you would otherwise miss. There is no camera on this site's product list with a verified listing, so this page does not link one. Any camera you already trust is enough to answer the question.

The AKC's sign list, for a dog who shows several of them on a regular basis, is pacing, whining, or trembling as you prepare to leave, excessive barking or howling, chewing or digging around doors and windows, house accidents, drooling or panting, and desperate attempts to escape confinement. The ASPCA adds self-injury to that last one: broken teeth, cut paws, damaged nails. One whine at the door is not the diagnosis. A dog who hurts itself trying to get out is.

## Why a Golden feels the door

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">The breed wants to be with you. Alone time is a skill, and it is taught in seconds before it is taught in hours.</div>

McConnell's reasons, as the AKC reports them, are practical. Some dogs were never left alone. Some lived through a traumatic separation, which she includes for certain shelter dogs. A single bad event while the owner was gone, such as a break-in, can be enough. Clingy individuals are at higher risk than independent ones. The AKC adds the life changes that show up in ordinary houses: a new work schedule, a move, a divorce, a death, a child leaving for college. It also notes research pointing at a lack of daily exercise as one possible contributor. Exercise does not cure panic. The AKC is explicit about that. A tired dog is simply more able to settle.

VCA's prevention note for puppies is the piece that belongs in the first week. Puppies need scheduled time alone, in their own crate or bed, and they should come back to the family when they are quietly playing, so the quiet behavior is the one that gets rewarded. That is the work in the [first week home guide](/guides/golden-retriever-first-week-home) and the age limits in the [alone time by age guide](/guides/golden-retriever-alone-time-by-age). How much running a Golden actually needs is in the [exercise guide](/guides/golden-retriever-exercise-needs). The walk only happens if the leash is usable, which is the [leash training guide](/guides/golden-retriever-leash-training-guide). Off-leash fetch is exercise only after the [recall guide](/guides/golden-retriever-recall-training-guide) says the dog comes back. Leaving that same dog in an apartment, with a hallway and neighbors on the other side of the wall, is the [apartment living guide](/guides/golden-retriever-apartment-living-guide).

If the distress is barking that also happens at passersby, the [barking guide](/guides/golden-retriever-barking-guide) is the other map. If the whole pattern is bigger than the door, start with the [behavior problems guide](/guides/golden-retriever-behavior-problems).

## Build the absence before you need it

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Departures stay boring. Returns stay boring. The good thing is the food toy, not the goodbye speech.</div>

The AKC's prevention list is the one to use while the dog is still fine. Teach the crate as a place that predicts chew toys and food-releasing toys, and watch whether the dog settles or escalates. Leave for a very short time and lengthen only while the dog stays comfortable. Desensitize the cues: pick up keys or put on a coat and then stay home. Pair those cues with a high-value treat so they predict food. Give age-appropriate exercise and a brain workout. Teach a stay that survives you leaving the room, which is the work in the [basic obedience guide](/guides/golden-retriever-basic-obedience-training-guide). Greet and leave without a scene. If you come home to a mess, do not punish it.

VCA's version of the same idea is a predictable day. The dog should be able to tell when attention, exercise, food, training, and a nap are coming. New toys come out when it is time to settle. For the last 15 to 30 minutes before a real departure, VCA says ignore the dog, and do the key-and-coat routine out of sight when you can. At the homecoming, ignore the dog until they settle. VCA's window for that is often 10 to 15 minutes. The attention the dog wants is the reward for lying down, not for hitting the door.

The ASPCA splits treatment by severity, and the split matters. A mild case may improve by pairing every departure with a food puzzle that takes about 20 to 30 minutes. The example they give is a KONG stuffed with something the dog actually wants. Moderate and severe cases need real desensitization: many absences of one to two seconds that do not produce anxiety, built slowly, with a stuffed toy added once the dog can handle five to ten seconds. The ASPCA's hard rule during that work is that the dog is not left alone except in those practice absences. A sitter, daycare, a ride along, or a changed schedule is part of the treatment, not a luxury. If the practice absence produces fear, the ASPCA says the procedure backfires. That is why they want a Certified Applied Animal Behaviorist (CAAB or ACAAB) or a board-certified veterinary behaviorist (Dip ACVB) designing it. A Certified Professional Dog Trainer can help when that person has specific experience treating fear with desensitization and counterconditioning. CPDT by itself does not require that experience.

## A crate only if the dog already rests in it

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">The crate is a rest place you have taught. It is not a box you hope will hold a panic.</div>

The AKC's crate section is worth quoting in spirit. Used well, a crate is a quiet place paired with food and chews. Some dogs feel safer in it when left. Others panic, and the symptoms get worse. The goal is not to crate a Golden all day as the treatment. VCA says crates for dogs with separation anxiety should be used with caution because escape attempts can cause serious injury. The ASPCA says to watch during crate training and during a short confinement while you are still home. Heavy panting, excessive salivation, frantic escape attempts, or persistent howling means that crate is the wrong plan. A baby gate on one room is the ASPCA's alternative.

## Food that keeps the mouth busy

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">The stuffed toy is a safety signal for a short, planned absence. It does not cover an eight-hour day.</div>

The ASPCA's mild-case step is a puzzle toy at every departure, something that takes the dog about 20 to 30 minutes, with a KONG named as the example. They also say chewing and licking have a calming effect, and that a meal or a smear of food in a puzzle can be part of ordinary enrichment, including when you leave. VCA's departure version is the same idea: a toy stuffed tightly enough that the dog is still working after you are gone. Frozen is their suggestion for making it last. Give the first ones while you are home. If the dog can break the rubber or swallow pieces, that toy does not get left behind.

A lick mat is the flat version of the same idea, and it fits the ASPCA's note on licking. This site does not have a lick mat image or a listing that could be checked against a live product page, so there is no card for one. Any mat you use should be the kind the dog cannot chew apart, and it should be tested while you are in the room.

## Somewhere to lie down

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">VCA's settle place is a bed or mat the dog has practiced on. The bed is not the treatment.</div>

VCA trains a down or a settle on a bed, mat, or crate, then lengthens it, then adds distance, including leaving the room. The bed is a location the dog already knows how to rest on. It does not change the emotion of a departure by itself.

A soft foam bed is a poor match for a dog who panics and chews. Swallowed foam is an emergency, not a training setback. ## Wraps, supplements, and medication

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Nothing in a bottle or a vest replaces the departure practice. Doses belong to the veterinarian who has examined the dog.</div>

The AKC says training and counterconditioning are sometimes not enough, and that medication is a veterinary decision. It tells owners to be especially careful with a young dog, and to ask the vet before any over-the-counter product, including when the dog is already on a prescription. On that same list the AKC includes pheromone collars or diffusers and compression shirts. Those are adjuncts. They are not a plan.

VCA's medication note is the boundary for this site. Two drugs, fluoxetine and clomipramine, have been tested and FDA-approved for use in dogs, and VCA says dogs in this kind of distress can improve faster when an appropriate medication is added to behavior work. Some dogs are treated daily and some as needed. The veterinarian examines the dog and decides whether either drug is appropriate. This page does not give a milligram amount or a schedule. If you want to know whether medication belongs in the plan, that conversation is with your veterinarian or a veterinary behaviorist, using the video of the dog alone.

## When to stop and call

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Blood, broken teeth, or a dog who cannot be left for ten seconds is an appointment, not another week of practice.</div>

Call a veterinarian first if the dog is hurting itself, if house soiling might be medical, if an older dog's behavior changed, or if you are considering any drug or supplement. VCA wants that exam before medication. The ASPCA wants it before you treat house soiling as a behavior problem.

Call a Certified Applied Animal Behaviorist or a board-certified veterinary behaviorist if the video shows distress on ordinary absences, if escape attempts have started, or if your own short sessions are making the dog worse. The AKC says the condition is not always preventable and that treatment gets complicated enough to need that help. It also reports McConnell's view that treatment has a high success rate when it is actually done. That is not a promise that a particular dog will be fine on a given date. It is a reason to start the right plan instead of waiting the problem out.

While you wait for the appointment, follow the VCA and ASPCA management rule. Do not leave the dog alone for the absences that produce the panic. A sitter, daycare, or a person who can stay is the bridge. The [puppy training schedule](/guides/golden-retriever-puppy-training-schedule) is useful for a youngster who is still learning ordinary alone time. It is not a substitute for a behaviorist once panic is in the video.
