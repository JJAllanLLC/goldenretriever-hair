# Golden Retriever Recall

</div>

A come cue asks a Golden Retriever to quit whatever they are doing and run to you instead. The [American Kennel Club](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/training/reliable-recall-train-dogs-to-come-when-called/) calls that a lifesaving skill, and it is honest about the odds: dogs are not robots, so "reliable" means you are nearly sure they will come, not that a guarantee exists. Stephanie Gibeault, MSc, CPDT, writing for the [AKC](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/training/why-wont-my-dog-come-when-called/), says even a well-trained dog will not be perfect, which is why off-leash time belongs in a safe place. The [RSPCA](https://kb.rspca.org.au/categories/companion-animals/dogs/training/how-can-i-teach-my-dog-recall-to-come-when-called) puts the same limit in one sentence: do not walk a dog off-leash near a road. Something unpredictable, a bang or a sudden scare, can send them into traffic.

This page is the indoor game, the long line, and the point at which the leash stays on. It is reward-based, which is the method the [ASPCA](https://www.aspca.org/about-us/aspca-policy-and-position-statements/position-statement-training-aids-and-methods) supports: food, praise, petting, and play, and no equipment that hurts. A Golden who already pulls on a walk needs the [leash guide](/guides/golden-retriever-leash-training-guide) before this one is useful outside. A Golden who panics when you leave the house needs the [separation anxiety guide](/guides/golden-retriever-separation-anxiety), because that is not a failed come cue.

Chasing can turn the miss into a game that runs toward the road. Go to the dog, clip the leash, and pay a simple behavior they already know, such as a sit.</div>

## Where you are allowed to test it

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Indoors, the dog can be loose in a quiet room. Outdoors, the line stays on until you would bet on the recall.</div>

The AKC's reliable-recall page says that if you are not confident, there is no rule that the dog must ever be off-leash. Keeping them leashed while you train is the responsible choice, including a decision to stay on-leash outside your own home and yard for good. That protects the dog, other dogs, people, and wildlife. The [exercise guide](/guides/golden-retriever-exercise-needs) is where off-leash fetch shows up as daily exercise, and it belongs there only after this cue holds in a legal, fenced, or otherwise safe place.

## Name, come, then a hand on the collar

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">The cue goes on a behavior the dog is already doing. Say "come" as they start toward you.</div>

Gibeault's order is the one to follow. Teach the name first, in a small quiet room or on a leash. Wait until the dog is looking somewhere else, say the name in a happy voice, and mark the look with "yes" or a click, then a treat. Add people and toys on the floor only after the look is eager.

Then teach a collar grab. Most dogs duck a hand that reaches for the neck, and an emergency recall is useless if they arrive and spin away. Lure them in with a treat they can lick but not take yet. Say "gotcha," take the collar gently, and then let them eat. Speed the grab up only after the slow one is comfortable. Pay every grab, in more than one room. The [basic obedience guide](/guides/golden-retriever-basic-obedience-training-guide) is the sit and stay that you add once the dog is arriving and standing still to be paid. Gibeault adds sit after the happy run is reliable, so the dog does not bounce off as soon as the food is gone.

The come cue itself starts a few feet away. Say the name, then "come," and use your body: pat your legs, clap, step backward. Mark the moment they move toward you, praise, and pay. The other AKC page says to show the treat first, and to add the word only when you are sure the dog is already on the way. After a few repetitions you can hide the food until they arrive. Run backward on the cue. Dogs chase a person who is leaving. The RSPCA uses the same move: a couple of paces away, a friendly voice, get low.

Call once. If you need the word three times, the AKC says the place is too hard or the skill is not there yet. Repeating "come, come, come" is also how a cue gets poisoned. When "come" already means nothing, or it means the fun is over, the AKC's fix is a new word, such as "here" or "close," trained from the first indoor step again. A whistle can be that new sound, because it has not been used as a scolding. This site has no whistle image and no listing that could be checked against a live product page, so there is no whistle card. Any cue you pick still has to be paid, and it still has to be one sound, said once.

## Pay them like you mean it

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Easy repetitions can be a small treat. A turn away from a real distraction gets the food you save for that moment.</div>

Gibeault says the reward might be a game of tug or a piece of chicken, and that this is not the time to be stingy. The AKC's reliable-recall page names chicken, cheese, or freeze-dried beef liver for the moment the dog gets to you. Praise, petting, and play count too. The ASPCA lists those alongside food as the core of humane training.

Two foods on this site's [training list](/products?category=Training) match that split. The beef liver is the high-value pay for a hard recall. The small chicken treats are for the many easy ones indoors, so you can pay often without turning every repetition into a meal of liver. A treat pouch is the practical way to keep either food in reach on a walk. There is no pouch image in the repo and no verified pouch listing, so this page does not link one. A pocket works if the dog cannot rob it.

The liver below is the freeze-dried beef liver already on the product list. The live page is a 21-ounce tub of Stewart freeze-dried treats made from 100 percent beef liver. The AKC names that kind of food as a high-value recall reward. Follow the bag for how much to feed. Give the first pieces while you are home so you know the dog chews them rather than swallowing them whole.

For a dog who cares more about a toy than about food, the RSPCA says to put that toy away and bring it out only for training. A round of tug when they arrive is a legal version of Gibeault's reward. Do not play tug on the leash. That habit is covered in the leash guide.

## The long line is a fence you can carry

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Outside, start on about 6 feet. Move to a 20-foot or 30-foot line. The line prevents a bolt. It does not drag the dog to you.</div>

Gibeault's outdoor sequence is specific. Do not let the dog roam free just because indoor recall looks finished. Repeat the same steps on a 6-foot leash. Then use a 20-foot or a 30-foot long line, and give more of that distance only as the recalls keep working. If the dog does not come, walk toward them while you gather the slack, and set the picture up again. The [RSPCA's UK page](https://www.rspca.org.uk/adviceandwelfare/pets/dogs/training/come) says the same tool in fewer words: a long training lead lets the dog have some freedom without free range.

This site does not have a long-line photo or a listing that could be checked against a live product page, so there is no long-line card. Buy a line in the length the AKC names, 20 or 30 feet, and handle it so it cannot cinch a hand or wrap a leg. If the dog is likely to hit the end at a run, clip it to the harness from the [leash guide](/guides/golden-retriever-leash-training-guide). The RSPCA's leash-equipment page says a harness keeps a pull off the neck. A long line used for a pre-walk sprint is a different session from a recall lesson. Mixing them is how the leash guide says a training walk turns into a tow.

Three games from the AKC keep the cue from meaning "the fun is over":

- Catch me. On leash, get their attention, turn, and run a few steps, then say the cue as they move with you. Stop and pay. Know they are watching before you move, so the leash does not yank them.
- Find me. Call from another room once the house recall is real. Pay when they find you.
- Hot potato. Two or more people stand apart with the good food and take turns calling. Pay every arrival, then send the dog back to play.

The RSPCA UK adds the rule under those games. Do not use the cue only to clip the leash and go home. Call in the middle of a good time, pay, and release them to the game again. The AKC says to do that a few times in one play session.

## When come is the wrong project

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">A dog who will not leave your side, and a dog who vanishes after a squirrel, are two different problems.</div>

A Golden who sticks to you in the house and falls apart when you pick up keys is not showing a recall failure. That pattern is the [separation anxiety guide](/guides/golden-retriever-separation-anxiety). Off-leash running is also the exercise that guide allows only after the dog actually comes back, and only for a dog who can be left without panic. Do not practice recalls by calling the dog in order to shut them away.

A dog who blows off come around other dogs, wildlife, or a crowd needs an easier picture, not a harsher one. The ASPCA's position is that training should not inflict distress, and its [behavioral help page](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/general-pet-care/behavioral-help-your-pet) puts "come when called" in group class for ordinary manners. Chase that has become dangerous, or a dog who panics, is the kind of problem that page sends to a qualified trainer or behavior professional you have interviewed about methods. The ASPCA also says only a veterinarian diagnoses illness or pain. If an adult who used to come has stopped, and they seem sore, lost, or suddenly unwilling to move, book the clinic before you add distance.

Puppy recall starts in the house, with the same short game, during the routines in the [puppy training guide](/guides/puppy-training) and the [socialization guide](/guides/golden-retriever-socialization-guide). An adult can learn it too. You may be undoing a cue that already means "I can ignore that word." Change the word, go back to a few feet, and keep the line on outside.
