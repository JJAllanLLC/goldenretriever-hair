# Golden Retriever Allergies

</div>

A Golden Retriever who cannot stop scratching needs a cause before a new bottle. Allergy is the pattern this breed is known for, and infection often follows the scratching. The [American Kennel Club](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/dog-skin-allergies/) calls skin allergy the most common allergic reaction in dogs, and it names three causes: fleas, food, and atopic (environmental) allergy. [VCA Animal Hospitals](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/atopy-in-dogs) lists the Golden Retriever among the breeds prone to atopic dermatitis, the itchy skin disease set off by pollens, molds, and dust mites. The [Merck Veterinary Manual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/integumentary-system/atopic-skin-conditions-in-dogs-and-cats/atopic-dermatitis-in-dogs) puts the same breed on its predisposition list. The characteristic sign is itch, often starting in a young adult, commonly on the paws, face, ears, and underside.

Skin allergies, food reactions, and the scratching that shows up after dark are all on this page. It is not a diagnosis, and it does not give drug doses. Paws that are the whole story stay in the [licking paws guide](/guides/golden-retriever-licking-paws-constantly). Rashes that are not an allergy pattern stay in the [skin problems guide](/guides/golden-retriever-skin-problems-guide).

## Read the itch in front of you

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Where the dog chews is a clue. It is not a diagnosis. VCA treats atopy as a diagnosis of exclusion.</div>

[VCA's atopy page](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/atopy-in-dogs) is explicit that there is no single test for environmental allergy. The signs match parasites, infections, and food reactions. The workup rules those out. Intradermal and blood allergy tests come after that clinical diagnosis, and they are for building immunotherapy, not for proving the dog is allergic. A blood panel ordered from the couch does not replace the exam.

Age of onset is not one number. [VCA's general allergy page](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/allergy-general-in-dogs) says most dogs with atopic dermatitis first show signs between one and three years. Its atopy page says signs often start between two and six, and it includes Golden Retrievers on the breed list. Merck's range is about 6 months to 3 years. A Golden who starts this as a young adult fits the pattern. A senior who is newly itchy needs the exam for infection, parasites, pain, and hormone disease, not a food aisle. The [shedding guide](/guides/golden-retriever-shedding-guide) is what that itch does to the coat.

## What the veterinarian is sorting

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Parasites and infection come off the list first. Then a food trial. Then, if it is still itch, the environmental plan.</div>

VCA's atopy workup looks for fleas and mites, bacterial infection, and ringworm, and it may include a biopsy when the skin could be autoimmune. It may also include bloodwork and a thyroid test before a dog is put on long-term medication. A food trial is how food is ruled in or out. For those 8 to 12 weeks the dog eats the prescribed diet and nothing else. Improvement points at food. No improvement, on a trial that was actually strict, points away from the bowl. Merck says the diet is confirmed only when the old food brings the signs back.

Allergy shots, which VCA calls hyposensitization, are built from the test results after the diagnosis exists. The goal VCA describes is less itch and less reliance on medication, not a cure. Its general allergy page says symptoms can be controlled and a permanent cure is not typical.

The [American College of Veterinary Dermatology](https://acvd.org/faqs/) is the referral step when the primary clinic is stuck. ACVD lists these signs: constant licking, chewing, or scratching, lots of head shaking, hair loss, a change in the color or texture of the skin, or a bad odor from the skin or ears. A dermatologist does not replace your veterinarian. ACVD says the specialist handles the skin, allergy, or ear plan and your clinic stays the primary doctor.

## Food allergy is a protein problem

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Beef, dairy, and chicken lead the published lists. Grain-free does not remove them. The trial is one diet, then the old food on purpose.</div>

[Merck's food-allergy page](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/integumentary-system/food-allergy/cutaneous-food-allergy-in-animals) lists the common food allergens in dogs as beef, dairy products, chicken, wheat, and lamb. Less common on that table are soy, corn, egg, pork, fish, and rice. [VCA's food-allergy page](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/food-allergies-in-dogs) names proteins especially, and lists dairy, beef, chicken, chicken eggs, soy, and wheat gluten. It also says virtually any ingredient can do it. [VCA's puppy page](https://vcahospitals.com/pediatric/puppy/health-wellness/food-allergies-in-puppies) puts beef, dairy products, and chicken as the top three, and says grains are not usually responsible, though some dogs are allergic to wheat. Those lists describe dogs in general. They do not diagnose the Golden in your kitchen. A dog can react to more than one food, Merck notes, and the common ones tend to be the ones dogs are fed most often.

A food allergy and a food intolerance are not the same complaint. Merck defines a hypersensitivity as an exaggerated immune response to something that should have been tolerated. It files other diet problems separately: metabolic trouble such as lactose intolerance, reactions to compounds in the food, and true toxins. Together those are adverse food reactions. An owner often calls all of them an allergy. Itch, repeat ear infections, and sometimes vomit or diarrhea can come from the immune kind. A soft stool after a rich meal is not, by itself, that diagnosis.

The diets used to test this are veterinary diets, not a limited-ingredient bag from the grocery aisle. VCA describes three:

- A hydrolyzed protein diet. The proteins are broken into pieces too small for the immune system to recognize. VCA's examples are Hill's Prescription Diet z/d, Royal Canin Hypoallergenic Hydrolyzed Protein or Anallergenic, and Purina Pro Plan Veterinary Diets HA Hydrolyzed.
- A novel-protein diet. It leaves out ingredients the dog has already eaten. VCA's examples include Hill's Prescription Diet d/d and Royal Canin Selected Protein. [VCA's elimination-trial page](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/implementing-an-elimination-challenge-diet-trial-dog) says the ideal version is one protein and one carbohydrate the dog has never had, such as rabbit, venison, or duck, chosen from the diet history.
- A home-cooked novel-protein diet, written by a veterinary nutritionist and balanced with a supplement the nutritionist specifies. Do not invent the recipe.

VCA says a hydrolyzed diet is the practical choice when nobody can reconstruct what the dog has eaten. A novel protein only works when that history is known. Dogs who do poorly on one hydrolyzed diet sometimes do better on a different one, so the clinic may run more than a single trial. Retail foods that say "limited ingredient" are not held to the same cross-contamination controls as those veterinary diets, on VCA's account. A flavored chew, a pill pocket, a leftover, or a chewable heartworm tablet can carry the old protein back in. Merck says the allergen is often hiding in treats, supplements, and flavored medication.

The elimination itself is the 8 to 12 weeks VCA describes. If the itch settles, the next step is the challenge, not a lifetime of guessing. VCA says to put the old food back. If the signs return within a week, that page calls the food allergy confirmed. Merck wants the same rechallenge for a different reason: improvement during the trial can be the season changing, better flea control, or wishful scoring. A dog who improves and does not flare on the old food is not counted as food allergic. The flare is often within days, sometimes within hours, and Merck says it can take 10 to 14 days to show. Test one food at a time, no more often than every two weeks, so you know which item did it. When the itch returns, go back to the trial diet. If the trial only helped partway and the old food still makes it worse, Merck reads that as food plus a second problem, often environmental allergy or fleas.

## Grain-free is not the treatment

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Taking the grain out leaves beef, dairy, and chicken in the bowl. It is not a food trial.</div>

Wheat is on the allergen lists. "Grain" as a category is not. A grain-free bag can still be built on chicken or beef, which is the opposite of removing the proteins Merck and VCA name. [Tufts Cummings](https://vetnutrition.tufts.edu/2021/07/the-role-of-carbohydrate-in-pet-foods/) says food allergies are more often to animal ingredients than to plant ingredients, and that there is little evidence that carbohydrate in a complete, balanced food is harmful to dogs.

The heart question is a separate one, and it is not settled. In July 2018 the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/outbreaks-and-advisories/fda-investigation-potential-link-between-certain-diets-and-canine-dilated-cardiomyopathy) began investigating reports of dilated cardiomyopathy, a serious heart disease, in dogs eating certain foods. Many of those foods were labeled grain-free and listed peas, lentils, other legumes, or potatoes among the main ingredients. In the label review through April 2019, more than 90 percent of the reported products were grain-free, and 93 percent contained peas and/or lentils. The FDA's [questions and answers](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/animal-health-literacy/questions-answers-fdas-work-potential-causes-non-hereditary-dcm-dogs) also say reports have involved grain-containing diets, that pulses are not known to be inherently dangerous, and that the agency does not know the specific connection. Report counts, the FDA says, do not by themselves prove a cause. It has not recalled foods over this, because it does not have definitive information that the diets are inherently unsafe. In December 2022 it said it would not post another public update until there was meaningful new science. Ask the veterinarian before a diet change. A grain-free switch is not how an itchy Golden gets diagnosed.

## Night is when you hear it

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Bedtime scratching is a clue about the day the dog just had. Treat the cause, not the clock.</div>

Owners notice the itch at night because the dog is finally still and the house is quiet. Pollen, grass, and dust that rode in on the coat and paws are still there. VCA's treatment list includes bathing because it removes allergens from the coat. A rinse or wipe of the paws and the lower legs after the walk is the small version of that, on a dog whose skin is intact. It does not fix an infection.

## What actually changes the itch

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">The clinic picks the drug and the amount. Baths remove allergens. Fatty acids are an extra, only if your veterinarian wants them.</div>

VCA's general allergy page groups treatment without pretending one product covers every dog. Anti-inflammatory drugs, including corticosteroids, are often used for a sudden flare. Antihistamines sometimes help mild signs. Newer drugs block specific itch signals: VCA names oclacitinib (Apoquel), ilunocitinib (Zenrelia), and the injection lokivetmab (Cytopoint). Those names are examples. They are not a shopping list. The veterinarian chooses the drug, if any, and sets the amount and the schedule.

Baths are the part you can see. VCA says regular bathing with a medicated or hypoallergenic shampoo can soothe the skin and lift allergens off the coat, and that antibacterial or antifungal shampoos are for secondary infection. Frequency and contact time belong to the label the clinic handed you. An oatmeal shampoo is a gentle option for mild dryness. Do not substitute a grocery shampoo, a human shampoo, or an essential oil for a prescription.

## When to stop and call

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Odor, hair loss, a wet patch, or a face that is swelling is an appointment. Trouble breathing is an emergency.</div>

Call the clinic promptly for itch that is constant, skin that is red or raw, a bad smell, hair loss, darkened or thickened skin, repeat ear trouble, or a dog who cannot sleep. That list matches the ACVD signs for a dermatology visit and the pattern VCA says comes with chronic infection: dark, thickened skin, saliva staining, and sores the dog makes by scratching.

Go the same day for a hot, moist lesion, pus, blood, a dog who will not let you touch an ear, or swelling of the face or muzzle. Trouble breathing, collapse, or hives that are spreading is an emergency, not a bath.

While you wait, keep the dog from chewing open skin, skip new foods and new supplements, and bring a short video of the nighttime scratching plus a list of everything the dog eats, including treats. The skin problems guide is the wider map if the coat disease does not look like itch-first allergy. The licking paws guide is the page when the feet are the injury you can see.

## Questions owners ask

### What is the most common allergy in Golden Retrievers?

The one this breed is listed for, by both VCA and Merck, is environmental atopy: pollens, molds, and dust mites, showing up as itch on the paws, face, ears, and underside. The AKC still names fleas, food, and environment as the three causes of skin allergy, and the exam takes them off the list in that order. There is no Golden-only ranking that lets you skip the flea check or the food trial.

### Can a Golden Retriever be allergic to chicken?

Yes. Chicken is on the common-allergen list at both VCA and Merck, along with beef and dairy. That is a reason to tell the veterinarian what the dog eats. It is not a reason to throw out the chicken and call it solved. Only the elimination diet, followed by a flare when that food comes back, confirms it.

### Does grain-free food help a dog with allergies?

Not as a treatment. Grains are not the usual allergen. Wheat can be, which is one ingredient, not a reason to ban every grain. A grain-free food often still contains chicken or beef, and it may replace the grain with peas, lentils, or potatoes. The FDA has been investigating a possible association between some of those diets and dilated cardiomyopathy. It has not established a cause, and it has not recalled the foods. The food trial uses the diet the veterinarian prescribes.

### What is the difference between Apoquel and Cytopoint?

VCA describes oclacitinib (Apoquel) as a daily oral medication and lokivetmab (Cytopoint) as a long-acting injection. Both block specific itch signals. It also names ilunocitinib (Zenrelia) as another daily oral option. Which drug fits, and the amount, is the veterinarian's decision. Neither one replaces flea control, a food trial, or treatment of the infection that scratching started.

### Why is the itching worse at night?

The itch was usually there all day. Night is when the house is quiet and the dog is still, so you hear the chewing. Pollen and dust are still on the coat. Wipe the paws after the walk, on skin that is intact. If the dog is breaking skin or cannot settle, that is a clinic visit, not a later bedtime.

### Will a Golden Retriever outgrow allergies?

Do not plan on it. VCA says the signs of environmental allergy can be controlled and that a permanent cure is not typical. A confirmed food allergy is managed by avoiding that food, not by waiting for the dog to age out of it. Dogs do get more comfortable when the fleas, the infection, and the right long-term plan are actually in place.

### How long does a food trial take?

VCA uses 8 to 12 weeks of one diet, with no treats or flavored extras. If the dog improves, the old food goes back in on purpose. VCA looks for a return of signs within a week. Merck says the flare can take as long as 10 to 14 days, so one new ingredient at a time is the pace. Improvement without that flare does not confirm a food allergy.
