# Golden Retriever Paw Care: Checks, Salt, Heat and Cracked Pads

</div>

A Golden Retriever's paws take the whole job: the fetch on the lawn, the swim, the trail, the salted sidewalk in January. Most paw problems start small. A grass seed between the toes, a crack in a pad, a burn from a summer sidewalk, or a coat of de-icer the dog licks off later. Catching them early is mostly a two-minute habit after each walk.

The breed adds a twist that generic paw articles skip. Goldens grow fur between their pads and around their toes, and that fur holds snow, burrs, and grit. The [AKC breed standard](https://images.akc.org/pdf/breeds/standards/GoldenRetriever.pdf) describes the ideal Golden foot as round and compact with thick pads, and it allows excess hair on the feet to be trimmed. The [American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA)](https://www.avma.org/resources-tools/pet-owners/petcare/cold-weather-animal-safety) says clipping the hair between a dog's toes may reduce ice balls. So the routine here is simple: look, wipe, dry, trim the fur, keep the nails short, and adjust for the season.

## Check your Golden's paws after every walk

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">A two-minute look at the pads, between the toes, and the nails catches most problems while they are still small.</div>

The best paw care routine is a quick check after each walk. Lift each foot, look at the pads, spread the toes, and look at the nails and dewclaws. Feel each foot for heat or swelling compared with the others. The [American Animal Hospital Association (AAHA)](https://www.aaha.org/resources/how-to-protect-dog-paws-from-hot-pavement/) says to inspect your dog's paws regularly because early detection can prevent more serious problems.

What you are looking for:

- Redness on the pads or between the toes
- Cracks, flaps, cuts, or bleeding
- Something stuck: a burr, a stone, glass, a grass seed, or a tick
- Swelling, heat, a bad smell, or discharge
- Licking or chewing at one foot
- Limping, or refusing to walk on a surface

If your Golden starts limping without an obvious cause, [VCA Animal Hospitals](https://vcahospitals.com/pediatric/puppy/health-wellness/dog-paw-injuries-and-how-to-help) says the first thing to do is check the bottom of the foot, because the cause can be something as simple as gum or tape stuck to the fur. In winter, the AVMA adds that a sudden limp on a walk may be an injury, or it may be ice packed between the toes.

Make the check pleasant. Handle the feet when the dog is relaxed, praise, and hand out a treat for each paw. A Golden who is used to having its feet touched is much easier to help on the day something is wrong.

## Trim the fur between the pads, not the pads

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Trim the fur that grows past the pads so it can't hold snow, burrs, and grit, and keep the foot's natural round shape.</div>

Yes, you should trim the hair between a Golden's paw pads when it grows long. Long fur between the pads collects debris and snow, and it can make a dog slide on smooth floors. The AVMA's cold-weather advice is that clipping the hair between the toes may reduce ice balls. The AKC breed standard says the feet may be trimmed and stray hairs neatened, as long as the natural outline is not changed.

How to do it at home:

1. Use blunt-tipped grooming scissors or a small clipper made for paws.
2. Have your dog lie down or stand on a non-slip surface.
3. Spread the toes gently and trim the fur level with the pads. Don't dig into the creases between them.
4. Trim fur that sticks out past the outline of the foot so it looks round and compact.
5. Go slowly, give treats, and stop if your dog gets worried. Finish another day.

How often depends on how fast your dog's coat grows. Trim when the fur starts to cover the pads, trap debris, or cause slipping. Many owners do it at the same time as the nails. If you are nervous about scissors near the pads, ask a groomer to show you once, then keep it up at home.

## Keep the nails short

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Short nails don't catch and tear, and they can't curl into the pad.</div>

Nails are part of paw care because long ones cause paw injuries. VCA says overgrown nails are more likely to catch and tear, and that they can grow so long they curl into the paw pad and create a painful wound. A torn nail that reaches the quick can bleed a lot. In VCA's [summer paw problems article](https://vcahospitals.com/resources/conditions-dog/skin-coat/prevent-these-five-common-summer-paw-problems), the advice is to keep nails trimmed short so they don't snag during play or a full-speed run.

Many owners go by sound: nails clicking on hard floors are a cue to check length. Technique, how short to go, and how to deal with a dog who hates nail trims are in the [nail trimming guide](/guides/golden-retriever-nail-trimming-guide). Clippers and grinders both work. A grinder files the nail down a little at a time, and Amazon's review summary for the grinder below says many customers prefer it to clippers.

## Hot pavement can burn your dog's pads

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">If you can't hold the back of your hand on the ground for seven seconds, it is too hot for your Golden's feet.</div>

Hot surfaces can burn a dog's paw pads. The AVMA's [warm-weather advice](https://www.avma.org/resources-tools/pet-owners/petcare/warm-weather-pet-safety) is to avoid hot surfaces such as asphalt, and to take walks, hikes, and runs during the cooler hours of the day. AAHA gives the test: put the back of your hand on the surface for seven seconds, and if you can't keep it there, it's too hot for your pet. AAHA also points out that sand, metal, concrete, and artificial turf can get dangerously hot in direct sun, and that grass and dirt trails are generally cooler.

In summer:

- Walk early in the morning or late in the evening.
- Pick shaded routes, grass, or dirt for fetch.
- Test the ground with the back of your hand before you set off.
- Consider well-fitted booties or paw wax for walks you can't move off hot ground.

VCA describes burned pads as swollen, red, and blistered. If you think your dog burned its paws, AAHA says to get off the hot surface right away, move to shade or air conditioning, and rinse the paws gently with cool water. Don't use ice or very cold water. VCA lists any burn as a reason to see your veterinarian, and AAHA says severe burns need professional care.

## Winter salt, de-icers, and ice balls

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">In winter, wipe the feet, legs, and belly after every walk, and keep your Golden away from de-icers and antifreeze.</div>

Road salt and de-icers can hurt a dog in two ways: on the paws, and in the stomach once the dog licks them off. The AVMA says dogs can pick up de-icing products, antifreeze, and other chemicals on their feet, legs, and belly, and that de-icers can make a pet sick if swallowed. It recommends wiping or washing those areas when you get home, using pet-safe de-icers on your own property, and cleaning up antifreeze spills quickly, because even small amounts of antifreeze can be deadly.

Ice is the other winter problem. [VCA's foot pad first aid page](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/first-aid-for-torn-or-injured-foot-pads-in-dogs) says pads can be injured on icy surfaces as well as hot sidewalks, and advises avoiding icy or salt-covered sidewalks and roads in winter. The AVMA lists cracked or bleeding pads as signs of cold-weather damage, and says to check your dog's paws often.

For a Golden in snow:

- Keep the fur between the pads trimmed so snow can't ball up.
- Wipe and dry the feet, legs, and belly after every walk.
- Walk around visible salt when you can.
- Shorten walks in very cold weather, as the AVMA advises.
- Stay off frozen ponds and lakes. The AVMA warns you can't know whether the ice will hold your dog.

Coats, cold limits, and the rest of the season are in the [cold-weather guide](/guides/golden-retriever-cold-weather-guide).

## Booties and paw wax help only if they fit the job

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Use booties or paw wax for salt, heat, or rough ground you can't avoid, and get your dog used to them at home first.</div>

Booties are for specific conditions, not every walk. They earn their place on salt-heavy streets, long winter walks, hot ground you can't route around, and rough rocky trails. The AVMA says that if you use booties, make sure they fit properly. AAHA suggests well-fitted booties or paw wax for hot ground. It says to introduce booties gradually indoors and to check often for rubbing or discomfort.

Paw wax is the option for a dog who won't keep booties on. It is a thin layer on the pads, not a cure for an injury. If a pad is already cracked, bleeding, or raw, see the sections below and ask your vet what to put on it.

- Booties: measure the foot and follow the maker's size chart, then practice indoors with treats.
- Paw wax: a thin layer, rubbed in. Musher's Secret's own directions say that if it comes off on floors, you are using too much.
- Either one: take it off and look at the feet after the walk.

## Cracked or dry paw pads

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Rough, dry pads are common. Deep cracks, bleeding, or a limp mean the pad hurts and needs a vet.</div>

Cracked paw pads have a short list of common triggers: hot pavement, ice, salt and chemicals, rough ground, and a lot of running on hard surfaces. VCA's foot pad page names hot sidewalks, icy surfaces, and chemicals. VCA's [pododermatitis page](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/pododermatitis-in-dogs) lists trauma, excessive exercise, and running on hard surfaces among the causes of irritated, inflamed paws. The same page also lists allergies, infections, hormonal disease, and immune-mediated disease as causes. That is why cracking on several feet, or cracking that keeps coming back, is worth a vet visit even if your dog seems fine.

If a pad is only rough and your dog isn't bothered, rinse off salt or chemicals, dry the feet, keep the dog off the surface that caused it, and mention it at the next vet visit. A crack that is deep, bleeding, or making your dog limp is an injury. VCA says even minor foot pad injuries can be stubborn, and it recommends taking your dog to a veterinarian. Ask your vet before putting any cream or ointment on a cracked pad, because your dog will lick it.

## Cuts, torn pads, and things stuck between the toes

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Rinse it, remove only what comes out easily, press on any bleeding, and call your vet.</div>

A torn or cut pad needs a vet, and first aid holds things until you get there. VCA explains that a torn foot pad doesn't hold stitches well, that walking on it often reopens the wound, and that cuts and punctures there take longer to heal. Its first aid steps:

1. Look for glass, metal, or other debris. If you can easily grasp it with tweezers, remove it gently. Swishing the paw in cool water or spraying it with a hose may help dislodge small particles.
2. If something is lodged deep, leave it alone. Digging makes the injury worse. Your vet can remove it, with sedation if needed.
3. Wash the wound with a mild antibacterial soap.
4. Press on the wound with a clean towel to stop bleeding. If you can't stop the bleeding within 10 to 15 minutes, VCA says it's an emergency.
5. Cushion the foot with non-stick gauze and wrap it from the toes up to and including the wrist or ankle. You should be able to slide two fingers under the wrap.
6. Change the bandage daily and keep it dry. VCA says swollen toes, a foul smell, or a moist discharge at a bandage change are reasons to call your veterinarian.

Grass awns, also called foxtails, are the stuck object to watch for after summer walks. VCA says these seed heads can work through the skin and move deeper into the tissue, and that they often end up between the toes. Its advice is to mow your lawn regularly and avoid tall grass on walks. The same article says ticks like to hide between the toes, which is another reason to spread them during your check. A Golden's foot fur hides both, so look, don't just feel.

## Paw licking, red feet, and allergies

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Licking one paw usually points to a problem in that paw. Licking several, with red skin or rusty-brown fur, can point to itch, and allergies are one cause.</div>

Constant licking at one foot is a reason to look at that foot. VCA's foot pad page says a dog that licks its pads or limps may have a pad that is torn, punctured, or burned. Check for a cut, a burn, or something stuck. Licking several feet, with red skin between the toes, can be itch. VCA's pododermatitis page says allergic paws are inflamed and itchy, often on the tops of the paws, with reddish brown staining of the fur from saliva or yeast. VCA's puppy paw article adds that the constant licking, biting, and scratching from allergies can set the paw up for a fungal or bacterial infection.

That is a vet visit, not a home fix. Your vet will look for the cause, which VCA says can include allergies, infection, parasites, hormonal disease, and immune-mediated disease. Licking that won't stop, red or raw skin, hair loss on the feet, swelling, or a smell are all reasons to book it. Why Goldens lick their paws, and how to tell boredom from itch, is covered in the [paw licking guide](/guides/golden-retriever-licking-paws-constantly).

## When a paw problem needs a vet

<div className="takeaway my-6 rounded-r-lg border-l-4 border-amber-600 bg-amber-50 px-5 py-4 text-xl font-semibold text-amber-950">Bleeding that won't stop, deep cuts, burns, embedded objects, and signs of infection all go to the vet.</div>

VCA lists these as reasons to take a dog's paw to the veterinarian:

- Bleeding that doesn't stop with pressure
- Deep punctures or cuts
- Any burn
- An object embedded deep in the pad or between the toes
- Signs of infection: redness, swelling, pus, or ongoing pain

Go to an emergency clinic if a bleeding pad won't stop within 10 to 15 minutes of pressure, as VCA advises. Call your vet too if your Golden won't put weight on the foot, if the limp lasts, or if paw trouble comes with itching or sores elsewhere on the body. VCA notes that vets ask about lesions elsewhere when they look for the cause of inflamed paws. When in doubt, a quick exam is safer than waiting a week to see if it heals.

### How often should I check my Golden Retriever's paws?

After every walk is a good habit, and it only takes a couple of minutes. The AVMA says to check paws frequently in cold weather, and AAHA says to inspect them regularly in summer. Check right away if your dog limps, licks one foot, or comes back from tall grass, salt, or hot ground.

### Should I trim the hair between my Golden's paw pads?

Yes, when it grows past the pads. Trimmed fur holds less snow and debris and gives better grip on smooth floors. The AVMA says clipping the fur between the toes may reduce ice balls. Trim it level with the pads and leave the pads themselves alone.

### What can I put on my dog's cracked paw pads?

For a deep, bleeding, or painful crack, see your vet first, because VCA recommends a vet visit even for minor pad injuries. For healthy pads that face salt or heat, AAHA mentions paw wax as protection. Use a product made for dogs, and ask your vet before putting anything on an open crack, since your dog will lick it.

### Can road salt hurt my dog's paws?

Yes. VCA advises avoiding salt-covered sidewalks and roads in winter, and the AVMA says de-icers can make a dog sick if swallowed. Wipe or wash the feet, legs, and belly after every winter walk, and use pet-safe de-icers at home.

### How hot is too hot for a dog to walk on?

Use AAHA's test: hold the back of your hand on the ground for seven seconds. If you can't, it's too hot. Walk in the cooler hours and choose grass or shade, as the AVMA advises.

### Why does my Golden Retriever keep licking one paw?

Licking one paw usually means something is bothering that paw: a cut, a burn, a crack, a grass seed, or a tick. Check between the toes and the pads. If you find nothing and the licking continues, or the skin turns red or raw, see your vet.
