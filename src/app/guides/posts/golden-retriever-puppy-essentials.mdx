<h2 id="1-crate-setup" className="text-4xl font-bold text-amber-900 my-12">1. Crate Setup</h2>

A folding crate can provide a managed place for naps and support house training when it is sized, introduced, and scheduled correctly. A crate is not a substitute for toilet breaks, exercise, social contact, or supervision.

**MidWest Homes for Pets iCrate Folding Dog Crate** is one divider-equipped option to compare for the first 30 days.

**What size crate should you get for a Golden Retriever puppy?**

Measure from the floor to the top of your dog's head or ears, whichever is higher, and from the nose to the base of the tail; then follow the manufacturer's allowance and current size chart. The dog should be able to stand without crouching, turn around, and lie comfortably. A divider can reduce the usable space for a growing puppy.

Vehicle fit is a separate measurement problem. A crate that fits the dog may not fit a particular cargo area, and a household wire crate should not be described as crash protection unless the exact model has appropriate evidence for that use.

<h2 id="3-feeding-essentials" className="text-4xl font-bold text-amber-900 my-12">3. Feeding Essentials</h2>

Start with the **exact food and amount the breeder or rescue has been feeding** unless your veterinarian recommends a change. Familiar food can reduce the number of variables during the move. If you change foods, transition gradually unless a veterinarian directs otherwise.

For any puppy food, confirm the nutritional adequacy statement on the **current bag or manufacturer page**. It should cover growth, including growth of large-size dogs (70 lb or more as an adult), when that is appropriate for your puppy. Compare calories, feeding directions, the manufacturer's nutrition expertise, and quality-control practices—not marketing terms such as “premium.” Persistent poor appetite, vomiting, diarrhea, or failure to grow needs veterinary advice rather than serial food switching.

## Common Mistakes New Golden Puppy Owners Make

- **Buying a cart of random cute stuff** instead of a **small stack** that covers sleep, potty, feeding, biting, and cleanup.
- **Skipping the crate** and then wondering why nights feel impossible.
- **Having no chew ready** when mouthing starts — you need something durable **in the room**, not in a closet.
- **Waiting for the first accident** to buy enzymatic cleaner — odors train repeat mistakes faster than lectures train puppies.
- **Dumping kibble out of its original bag** — keep the food and its lot information in the original bag, placed inside a clean, dry container if desired.

## FAQ

**What do I actually need before bringing home a Golden Retriever puppy?**  
A safe sleep setup, the puppy's familiar food, a toilet plan and enzyme cleaner, a puppy-appropriate supervised chew, and small training rewards. Pads, food storage, a slow bowl, and hair cleanup depend on your home and puppy.

**What is the most important thing to buy first?**  
For most homes: **a safe sleep area, the familiar food, and a toilet/cleanup plan**. Add a suitable supervised chew and training rewards.

**Do I need a crate for a Golden Retriever puppy?**  
A properly introduced crate can support safe sleep and house training, but it is not the only possible management setup. Size it from the dog&apos;s measurements and build positive associations rather than forcing confinement.

**What should I buy to stop puppy biting?**  
Choose a puppy-appropriate chew from the current age and weight label, supervise it, and redirect calmly. Hard adult nylon is not appropriate for every puppy and can damage teeth. Read [best chew toys for Golden Retriever puppies](/guides/best-chew-toys-golden-retriever-puppies) and [why your Golden puppy bites your hands](/guides/why-does-my-golden-retriever-puppy-bite-my-hands) for the full playbook.

**What food should I start with for a Golden Retriever puppy?**  
Start with the exact food from the breeder or rescue unless your veterinarian recommends otherwise. For a new food, verify the current label's growth adequacy—including growth of large-size dogs when applicable—compare calories and manufacturer practices, and transition gradually. Appetite or digestive problems that persist need veterinary advice.

**Are puppy pads worth it?**  
They are optional. Use them for an intentional indoor toilet area or when outdoor access is not possible; otherwise a consistent outdoor schedule may be clearer. See the [potty training schedule chart](/guides/golden-retriever-potty-training-schedule-chart).

## Conclusion

You do **not** need every Golden Retriever puppy product on the internet in the first 30 days. You **do** need a **short essentials stack** that covers **sleep, feeding, potty, biting, and cleanup** — then **consistency** with what you bought.

Start with a measured sleep setup, the puppy's familiar food, a toilet and cleanup plan, a safe supervised chew, and rewards. Add pads, storage, a slow bowl, and hair cleanup only when they solve a real need, and begin gentle grooming handling from the start.
